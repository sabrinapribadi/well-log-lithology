Well-Log Lithology

Does a rock-type model trained on North Sea wells hold up on wells it never saw, and does it transfer to Kansas?

1

Problem

Lithology is the rock type at each depth: sandstone, shale, limestone and nine others.

  • Petrophysicists pick it by hand from well logs: gamma ray, density, neutron, sonic, resistivity curves.
  • Across hundreds of wells, picks are slow and inconsistent.
  • Wrong picks flow into reservoir models and drilling decisions.
  • Public archives hold tens of thousands of wells nobody has interpreted.
Today vs this project
Load a well's logs→Pick rock type by eye, depth by depth→Repeat per well
Model labels every depth→Doubtful depths flagged→Petrophysicist reviews only those

What they need: first-pass picks for every well, with uncertainty, judged on wells the model never saw.

Label and test design
  • Label: 12 rock types, interpreted by the data providers, one per 0.152 m (6-inch) depth sample.
  • Test: 10 hidden wells, scored once per pre-named model; 10 open wells reported, never used for choices.
  • Leak guard: no well is split; neighbouring depths are nearly identical.
  • Metric: the competition's penalty score; geologically "far" mistakes cost more (how it works).

? How to read the penalty score
What: the average cost of a mistake per depth sample; 0 is perfect. Near mistakes (chalk for limestone) cost 1.375, far ones up to 4. Example: −0.540 means each 0.152 m sample costs 0.54 on average: roughly one "medium" mistake every four samples. So what: labelling everything shale scores ; the 2020 finalists scored −0.469 to −0.526.

What if the petrophysicist reviews only the doubtful depths?

Move the dial: flag every depth where the model's confidence is below it.

? How to read this
Confidence: the model's probability for its chosen rock (0–100%). Example: at 75%, about a quarter of the depth is flagged and most mistakes are inside it. So what: review effort can be set like a budget; the unflagged rest is more reliable.

Which rocks the model finds, on hidden wells

Share of each rock's metres labelled correctly (recall). Hover a bar.

? How to read this
Recall: of the metres the interpreter called this rock, the share the model also called it. Example: shale near 90% means 9 in 10 shale metres are found. So what: rare rocks (dolomite, chalk) need review; a few hundred metres cannot be judged reliably.

Caveats
  • Labels are interpretations, not core; interpreters disagree too.
  • Ten hidden wells: one unusual well moves the score.
  • Kansas transfer is not tested yet (P3); North Sea results only.
FAQ
Why not accuracy? Shale is 59–62% of samples; "all shale" is 59% accurate yet useless. Why not a random split? It scored , about 3× better than blind wells: a leak. Why not tune on hidden wells? Then the score would describe those 10 wells, not new ones.
